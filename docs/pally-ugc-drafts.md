## Ground rules for all 10

- **Label #ad** (or the platform's paid-partnership toggle) on anything posted to her account. Non-negotiable.
- **Pally lands around 20-30 s, once, as a friend tip.** MinoAI's UGC coaching: the best UGC borrows hooks from non-UGC viral talking-head videos and drops the product in around the 20-30 s mark, "as subtle as possible," framed like you're friends with the viewer. If Pally requires an ad-read line, tuck it into the moment it's actually relevant rather than a "this video is sponsored by" opener.
- **The psychology is the video; Pally is one example of using it.** Several drafts name Pally alongside a non-app tip so it doesn't read as the whole point.
- **Keep Pally out of You Got Played.** A sponsor in YGP either looks exempt from the critique or gets critiqued. These sit under "make your brain work for you."
- **Only show real use.** Film her actually texting Pally. If a feature doesn't work the way the draft says, change the draft, not the footage.
- **Open on her or the viewer's brain, never on the app.** Product-first openings underperformed (HeyClicky 0.4x, YGP Ep 1 0.6x).

## 1. Phone anxiety: Pally calls for me
*Builds on the pipeline idea "Phone anxiety: Pally calls the restaurant for me."*

- **Hook** (from [@hannahhhstudiesss](https://www.instagram.com/p/DVZ1ygdiUjX/): "Things I do that I thought were normal, but are actually just symptoms of ADHD.")
  Skeleton: "Things I do that I thought were normal, but are actually just ___."
  Hers: "Things I do that I thought were normal, but are actually just my brain trying to avoid a phone call."
- **Open:** thumb hovering over the call button for a restaurant, then she puts the phone face down.
- **Beats:** the list (rehearsing the call in my head, letting it go to voicemail then texting "sorry missed your call", ordering online even when it costs more) → spotlight effect: we think people notice our awkwardness way more than they do → **Pally (~20 s):** "so for the calls I genuinely don't care about, like booking a table or sitting through a phone menu, I just text Pally and it calls for me." Show the result text.
- **Honest payoff:** avoidance is what keeps the anxiety going, so let AI take the boring calls and keep one scary call a week for yourself.
- **Fact-check:** Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), the Barry Manilow t-shirt study: wearers guessed about half the room noticed, actual was roughly a quarter. Verify exact numbers. Avoidance maintaining anxiety is standard CBT (Clark & Wells 1995 on safety behaviours). Any Gen Z "never answer the phone" stat (e.g. the 2024 Uswitch survey) is unverified; find the primary release or cut it.

## 2. Why you can't sleep with tasks in your head
- **Hook** (from [@emonthebrain](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbtF_UThhBF/): "neuroscience fun fact of the day is that complaining judging or criticizing others can actually change your brain...")
  Skeleton: "___ fun fact of the day is that ___ can actually ___."
  Hers: "Cog sci fun fact of the day is that an unfinished to-do can actually keep your brain awake, and the fix isn't finishing it."
- **Open:** her in bed at 1am, eyes open, then grabs her phone.
- **Beats:** unfinished goals keep popping into your head → the twist: you don't have to finish it, you just have to make a specific plan for it → **Pally (~20 s):** "so now when I remember something at 1am I just text Pally 'remind me to email my TA at 10' and I can actually let it go."
- **Payoff:** your brain wants a plan, not a finished task.
- **Fact-check:** Masicampo & Baumeister (2011), "Consider it done!", JPSP. Avoid overselling the classic Zeigarnik effect; replications are mixed. Lead with the 2011 plan-making finding.

## 3. The planning fallacy before finals
- **Hook** (from [@samuelofmacedonia](https://www.tiktok.com/@samuelofmacedonia/video/7581672898369211662): "This is the number one reason you hate studying and can't lock in, coming from someone who graduated with a near 4.0 in neuroscience.")
  Skeleton: "This is the number one reason you ___, coming from someone who ___."
  Hers: "This is the number one reason you're always cramming the night before, coming from someone who studies cognitive science at UCLA."
- **Open:** her writing "finish essay: 2 hrs" on a sticky note, then a jump cut to it being dark outside.
- **Beats:** students in a study predicted when they'd finish their thesis and most missed by weeks, even their "worst case" → you plan the best case, not your past → **Pally (~25 s):** "what fixed it for me was having Pally ask me how long it actually took last time, so I stop planning for the version of me that doesn't exist."
- **Payoff:** plan from your track record, not your hopes. Time for December finals.
- **Fact-check:** Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994), JPSP. Commonly cited: average prediction ~34 days, actual ~55, under a third finished by their own estimate. Verify. Confirm Pally's memory can actually do the "last time" recall before scripting that line; if not, swap for a reminder.

## 4. Implementation intentions (the lazy-but-ambitious hack)
- **Hook** (from [@rory.bingethinking](https://www.tiktok.com/@rory.bingethinking/video/7699305411156937998): "Here's a shockingly effective brain hack for anyone who's lazy but ambitious. It's called the Bader-Meinhoff effect...")
  Skeleton: "Here's a shockingly effective brain hack for anyone who's lazy but ambitious. It's called ___."
  Hers: "Here's a shockingly effective brain hack for anyone who's lazy but ambitious. It's called an if-then plan."
- **Open:** her at the gym door or desk, "I'm here because of a text I set up three weeks ago."
- **Beats:** "I'll study more" doesn't work; "if it's 4pm on Tuesday, then I open my notes at Powell" does → across almost 100 studies, people who made if-then plans reliably followed through more → **Pally (~20 s):** "I set mine up as automations in Pally, so the 'if' part literally texts me."
- **Payoff:** you don't need more motivation, you need a trigger.
- **Fact-check:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) meta-analysis, 94 tests, d ≈ 0.65 (medium-to-large). Don't say "doubles"; say "a medium-to-large effect" or "across almost 100 studies it reliably helped."

## 5. Why hold music feels like forever
- **Hook** (from [@askcatgpt](https://www.instagram.com/p/DdXph_-CX5B/): "I hooked an AI agent up to a physical phone so I could call it to get stuff done.")
  Skeleton: "I ___ an AI ___ so I could ___."
  Hers: "I let an AI sit on hold for me so I could find out why hold music feels like forever."
- **Open:** her phone on speaker, hold music, her slumped on the desk. Cut to her texting Pally and walking away.
- **Beats:** unoccupied time feels longer than occupied time, and uncertain waits feel longer than known ones → that's why some lines tell you "you're number 4" and why elevators have mirrors → **Pally (~15-20 s, it's the open):** she gets the result text while doing something else.
- **Payoff:** the wait isn't the problem, the empty uncertain wait is. Works on lines, loading screens and delivery trackers too.
- **Fact-check:** Maister (1985), "The Psychology of Waiting Lines." The elevator-mirror story is an anecdote; frame it as "the story goes."

## 6. The ostrich effect and your 400 unread emails
- **Hook** (from [@itshannahzhang](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfSPuJSS82K/): "If you are a smart highly ambitious person who isn't as rich or successful as you want to be yet it might be because you need to care less about succeeding.")

- **Open:** her inbox badge, she swipes the app away.

- **Payoff:** it's not laziness, it's avoidance, and you can design around it.
- **Fact-check:** Karlsson, Loewenstein & Seppi (2009), Journal of Risk and Uncertainty. Check that Pally's important-message alerts work with her email before filming.

## 8. Writing it down makes you smarter (offloading)
- **Hook** (from [@jun_yuh](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcsZDmOhOrE/): "You'll blow up once you truly understand human behavior.")
  Skeleton: "You'll ___ once you truly understand ___."
  Hers: "You'll study so much better once you truly understand that your brain is bad at holding onto things."
- **Open:** her trying to memorise a list while three reminders go off in her head (text overlay).
- **Beats:** people who saved a file before studying a new one learned the new one better, because their brain stopped holding the old one → offloading frees up memory for what matters → **Pally (~20 s):** "that's why I dump every random task into Pally the second I think of it. It's not that I'm forgetful, I'm making space."
- **Honest beat:** offload the to-dos, not the thinking (ties to the AI-offloading series).
- **Fact-check:** Storm & Stone (2015), "Saving-enhanced memory," Psychological Science. Background: Risko & Gilbert (2016), "Cognitive offloading," Trends in Cognitive Sciences.

## 9. Hot take: decision fatigue probably isn't real (defaults are)
- **Hook** (from [@andrewcodesmith](https://www.instagram.com/p/DRSlbvIE5Ug/): "This week I'm at IBM. I might not get invited back. My hot take is that when a company says they're AI powered, whatever that means, they're pretty much always not.")
  Skeleton: "My hot take is that when ___ says ___, ___."
  Hers: "My hot take is that when someone says they're too tired to decide because of decision fatigue, the science is shakier than you think."
  (His was a labelled partnership too, #IBMpartner, and still a contrarian take. Good model for this one.)
- **Open:** her staring at a menu or at Netflix, frozen.
- **Beats:** the famous willpower-as-a-battery studies largely failed to replicate → what does hold up: defaults, whatever happens when you do nothing wins → organ donation opt-in vs opt-out → **Pally (~25 s):** "so instead of deciding every morning, I made the good thing my default. Pally sends my brief at 8 and I just follow it."
- **Payoff:** stop relying on willpower, change what happens by default. Strong credibility play for a cog sci student.
- **Fact-check:** Hagger et al. (2016) multi-lab replication of ego depletion found an effect near zero. Johnson & Goldstein (2003), Science, on organ donation defaults. Jachimowicz et al. (2019) meta-analysis on default effects. Don't say decision fatigue is "fake"; say the evidence is much weaker than its popularity.

### 12. Skit: "how do you remember everyone's birthday?"
- **Hook (hers, no close MinoAI match):** friend: "how do you remember literally everyone's birthday?" Her: "I don't. I outsourced that part of my brain."
- **Beats:** transactive memory: couples and close friends split who remembers what, and remember more together than strangers do → we now do it with tech too → cut to her phone, a Pally text: "Maya's birthday is tomorrow, she mentioned wanting that matcha place" → friend: "that's cheating" → her: "that's cognitive science."
- **Why it's subtle:** Pally is the punchline of a skit, which is the format MinoAI called out as not feeling like an ad.
- **Fact-check:** Wegner (1987) on transactive memory; Wegner, Erber & Raymond (1991), real couples outperformed paired strangers. Avoid the "Google effect" (Sparrow et al. 2011); it didn't hold up well in replication. Confirm Pally's memory feature can surface this before filming.

### 13. Quick test: what did you forget to do today?
- **Hook (hers, viewer-test format):** "Quick test. Without checking anything, name one thing you were supposed to do today."
- **Beats:** remembering to do something later is called prospective memory, and it fails constantly because nothing reminds you at the right moment → mid-sentence, her phone buzzes on camera. She reads it: "call the dentist." She laughs: "okay, that's literally what I'm talking about." → the fix is a cue at the right moment, not a better memory.
- **Why it's subtle:** the product interrupts the video like real life. It only says "Pally" if the notification shows it.
- **Fact-check:** Einstein & McDaniel (1990) on prospective memory; Gilbert (2015) on offloading intentions to reminders.

### 14. My phone's home screen, explained by a cog sci student
- **Hook (hers):** "Every app on my home screen is where it is for a psychological reason."
- **Beats:** tour of her real home screen: Instagram buried in a folder on page 3 (friction), Notes in the thumb zone (easy wins), Messages dock spot: "and my texts, because half my life runs through them, including my AI assistant, Pally" (one line, keep moving) → behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a prompt line up, so she designs the prompts.
- **Why it's subtle:** Pally is one tile in a tour. Strong psych-in-tech fit on its own.
- **Fact-check:** Fogg Behavior Model (Fogg 2009; B=MAP). Keep friction claims general.

### 15. Rating viral productivity hacks as a cog sci student
- **Hook** (from [@hormozi](https://www.instagram.com/p/DdCY2UGBjKb/), on-screen: "$250M/YR FOUNDER RATES PRODUCTIVITY HACKS FROM 1-10")
  Skeleton: "___ RATES PRODUCTIVITY HACKS FROM 1-10"
